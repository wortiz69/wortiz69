Solid Character Animation

A charming, loopable illustration with well-composed secondary motion. Strong emotional clarity. Gaps in anatomical coherence and timing polish hold it back from the top tier — addressable in one revision pass.

1 · Philosophy Consistency 7 / 10
The piece commits to a warm, illustrative aesthetic — earthy browns, playful motion, golden sparkles — and maintains it consistently.

✓ Color palette (#c8834a, #fdf6ec) is harmonious and reads immediately as "friendly pet illustration."
✓ The shadow-pulse correctly mirrors the jump arc, showing intent around physical grounding.
△ The sparkles feel tonally adjacent to a videogame reward, slightly at odds with the storybook warmth of the dog. Either commit fully to the playful/gamey register or replace sparkles with floating paw prints or hearts.
2 · Visual Hierarchy 8 / 10
The dog is clearly the hero. The gradient background successfully recedes; the ground-ellipse adds depth without competing.

✓ Eye lands first (correctly — it's the lightest value on the darkest area of the face).
✓ Collar + tag create a mid-tone anchor that breaks the body mass pleasingly.
△ The title "Jumping Dachshund!" and subtitle compete with the animation — same vertical axis, similar weight. Consider fading them to 40% opacity after 3s so the animation owns the space.
3 · Detail Execution 6 / 10
SVG construction is functional but shows shortcuts that a trained eye catches quickly.

✓ Layering order (tail → body → head → ears → legs) is correct z-order for the breed's silhouette.
△ Legs are rectangular stubs — dachshunds have distinctively short, bowed front legs. A slight outward curve on the front pair would read as anatomically dachshund rather than generic dog.
△ Ear flip direction is currently "outward" (ear-l rotates negative, ear-r rotates positive). For a forward-facing dog, ears would tilt backward during ascent due to air resistance — swap the rotation signs.
△ The body ellipse doesn't squash on landing. Adding a 5% scaleY squeeze (0.95) at the lowest keyframe and a 5% stretchY on peak ascent would add significant life with two lines of CSS.
4 · Functionality / Completeness 7 / 10
Runs self-contained in any browser, zero dependencies, sub-2KB — excellent delivery format.

✓ Loopable: all animations use infinite with matching durations (0.72s). No drift.
✓ Shadow and dog-wrap animations are synchronised correctly — the shadow bottoms-out precisely when the dog lands.
△ No reduced-motion media query. Users with vestibular disorders may find the continuous bounce triggering. Add @media (prefers-reduced-motion) to pause all animations.
△ Sparkle timing (--delay) is fixed — after 5 seconds the pattern becomes perceptible. Randomise on load with a short JS snippet.
5 · Innovation / Delight 8 / 10
This is where the piece earns its stars. Most pure-CSS animal animations are static poses; this one commits to five simultaneous, coordinated secondary motions: jump arc, ear flap, tail wag, leg compress, shadow scale.

✓ The cubic-bezier(.36,.07,.19,.97) on the jump adds a genuine snap-and-settle that reads as weight.
✓ Mouth + tongue on the snout read as panting happiness — emotionally successful at a glance.
△ Missed opportunity: no sound design stub. Even a muted boing.mp3 triggered on the peak of the jump arc (via JS timeout tracking animation iteration) would 10× the delight factor.

Prioritised Fix List

P1
Add squash & stretch on landing — .dog-wrap keyframe at 85%: add scaleX(1.06) scaleY(0.94), peak at 45%: scaleX(0.95) scaleY(1.05). ~4 lines of CSS.
P1
Flip ear rotation direction — In @keyframes ear-l, change rotate(-18deg) to rotate(12deg) (backward, not outward). Same for ear-r: 18deg → -12deg.
P2
Add prefers-reduced-motion — wrap all animation: declarations in @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) and add a static fallback.
P2
Fade title text — after 2.5s, transition .title and .subtitle opacity to 0.25 so the animation is the sole focus.
P3
Curved front legs — replace the front-leg <rect> elements with <path d="M53,78 Q46,90 50,100"> style paths for the characteristic dachshund bow.
P3
Randomise sparkle delays on load — 5-line JS snippet sets each .spark's --delay CSS var to Math.random()*1.5s on DOMContentLoaded.
